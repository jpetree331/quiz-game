Modern commercial quiz books should not be assumed to be public domain. A commercially republished government reference may also include separately protected editorial material.

The U.S. Copyright Office distinguishes [facts from the way those facts are expressed](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-protect.html). It also explains that [original selection and arrangement in a compilation can be protected](https://www.copyright.gov/comp3/chap500/ch500-identifying-works.pdf). Public knowledge is therefore not a blanket permission to copy quiz-book questions or reproduce a curated collection.

## Public-domain data versus free question banks

[Wikidata's structured data is CC0](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Licensing), making it a suitable source of public-domain factual data for future independently written questions. This does not cover every piece of prose on Wikidata or content on linked websites.

[Open Trivia Database uses CC BY-SA 4.0](https://opentdb.com/api_config.php). It is available under an open license, but is not a public-domain question bank. It was not imported. Likewise, the reference websites above are factual references, not a blanket claim that their wording or imagery is public domain.

For future additions, independently select a fact, verify it, write a fresh question and plausible distractors, and record provenance here. Do not import a book or scraped question bank on the assumption that factual subject matter makes its wording unrestricted.
